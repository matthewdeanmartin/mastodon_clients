How ratings are calculated

A rating on this site is arithmetic, not an impression. It is computed by code from the structured record for each client, and it is reproducible: anyone with the repository can recalculate every star on the site and get the same numbers. No language model assigns or adjusts a rating.

How unknown values are handled

A feature we have not established reads as unknown. Unknown values earn no points, so an incompletely researched client scores lower than a fully researched one with the same capabilities. That is a deliberate choice, and it has an obvious failure mode: it can make a little-known client look worse than it is.

To keep that visible rather than hidden, every client page and every directory row shows the number of unknown core features alongside the rating. A client rated three stars with six unknowns is telling you something different from a client rated three stars with none. Corrections that resolve an unknown are the most useful kind.

A feature recorded as not applicable is removed from the denominator entirely rather than counted against the client, because penalising a product for lacking something that cannot apply to it would be measuring the wrong thing.

Why half stars

Finer granularity would imply a precision the inputs do not support. The underlying data is a hand-maintained record of a few dozen facts; resolving it to a tenth of a star would be false confidence.

What the rating is not

It is not a recommendation, and one number cannot capture what different readers need. A reader who wants a client for a phone, a reader who wants a keyboard-driven deck, and a reader who wants something a newcomer can be handed are looking for three different things, and the same star count means something different to each of them.

That is why the directory filters and the audience pages exist, and why the default ordering of the directory is alphabetical rather than by rating. Sorting the whole field by stars is the least useful thing this data supports.
