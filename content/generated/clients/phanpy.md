<!-- section: summary -->

Phanpy is a Mastodon web client whose stated design is minimalist and
opinionated. It gives particular attention to conversation threading, and offers
a column layout alongside its ordinary reading view. It is maintained primarily
by a single developer.

<!-- section: who_is_it_for -->

Phanpy suits a reader who spends more time reading than posting, and who follows
conversations rather than skimming a timeline. The combination of a thread view,
a column layout, working filters, and notification filtering supports reading at
volume without losing the thread of a discussion.

It records full keyboard navigation and deliberate accessibility work, which
makes it worth trying for anyone who navigates without a mouse.

It is a weaker fit for someone whose priority is composing: scheduled posts are
absent and draft support is partial.

<!-- section: notable_features -->

Phanpy records support for nearly every core capability in this directory:
multi-account, lists, filters, push notifications, bookmarks, translation,
hashtag following, polls, post editing, account notes, and notification
filtering. Advanced search and a column layout are both present, as is
installability as a progressive web app.

Two are limited. Draft support is partial, and profile editing is partial.
Quote posts and scheduled posts are not supported.

<!-- section: deployment -->

Phanpy is available at a hosted instance and can also be deployed yourself. It
works against any Mastodon-compatible server, so no server deployment of your
own is required to use it.

<!-- section: source_and_licensing -->

The source is public and carries the MIT licence.

<!-- section: funding -->

Funded by donations, through a GitHub Sponsors page. As with any
donation-supported hosted instance, its continued availability depends on one
maintainer's willingness and ability to keep it running. Self-hosting removes
that dependency for anyone who would rather not carry it.

<!-- section: verdict -->

Phanpy covers more of the core feature set than most clients in this directory
and pairs that with recorded accessibility work and full keyboard navigation.
What it asks in exchange is acceptance of its opinions: the interface is shaped
by a particular view of how a timeline should be read, and there is no scheduled
posting. Readers whose priority is composition rather than reading will find it
thinner in exactly the place they care about.
